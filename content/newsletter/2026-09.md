## Welcome to September

September in the West Valley is a bit of a tease. The calendar says fall, but the thermometer usually disagrees for a few more weeks. Still, there is a change in the air. Labor Day marks the unofficial end of summer, monsoon season winds down by the end of the month, and many businesses start their push toward the end of the year.

Four months are left in 2026. That is plenty of time to make a real difference in your results, especially if you use your BNI chapter well. Now is the time to look at your referral goals, sharpen your weekly presentation and make sure the room knows exactly how to help you.

## Seasonal tips for the West Valley

**At home**

- **Plan a safe Labor Day.** Labor Day is Monday, September 7. If you are heading to the lake or the high country, check the forecast, bring extra water and leave early to beat the traffic.
- **Clean up after monsoon.** Wipe down window sills and ceiling fans, swap out your AC filter and give your patio a good rinse to clear away the dust.
- **Keep running the AC smart.** It is still hot. Set your thermostat a little higher when no one is home and use fans to stay comfortable.
- **Start fall yard planning.** If you overseed your lawn with winter grass, late September into October is when many West Valley homeowners start preparing.
- **Get ahead of the holidays.** Make a short list of home projects you want done before guests arrive, and book help now while schedules are open.

**For your business**

- **Plan your fourth quarter.** Write down your goals for October through December, including revenue targets, marketing and any year-end deadlines.
- **Look at your budget for next year.** September is a good time to start thinking about 2027 costs, pricing and hiring.
- **Review your marketing calendar.** Plan your holiday messages and promotions now so you are not rushing in November.
- **Check in with your key clients.** A quick call to ask how things are going can lead to repeat business and referrals before the busy season.

## Networking tip of the month: tune up your weekly presentation

If your weekly presentation has sounded the same for months, the room may have stopped hearing it. Pick one specific thing to ask for each week. Name the type of client, the problem you solve and, if you can, a specific person or company you would like to meet. A clear, specific ask gives members something they can act on. Then switch it up the next week so people start to see the full range of what you do.
