Some of the best marketing partners for your business are not your competitors. They are the businesses that serve the same customers you do, at the same moments, with different services. A realtor and a mortgage lender. A roofer and a gutter installer. A wedding photographer and a florist.

When two complementary businesses work together on a joint offer, both get in front of new customers, share the cost of marketing and give customers a smoother experience. Here is how to make it work.

## What is a joint offer?

A joint offer is a promotion, package or event that two or more businesses put together. Some common forms:

- **Bundled services.** A home cleaner and a carpet cleaner offering a move-out package.
- **Cross-promotions.** Each business shares the other's offer with its own customers.
- **Co-hosted events.** A workshop, open house or seminar with multiple local experts.
- **Referral packages.** A "new homeowner kit" with offers from several trusted local businesses.

## Find the right partner

The best partners share three things with you:

1. **The same customers.** You both serve the same kind of person, whether that is homeowners, new parents, small business owners or retirees.
2. **The same timing.** They need both services at around the same time.
3. **The same standards.** You trust them to treat your customers the way you would.

That third point matters most. When you partner with someone, your reputation becomes linked to theirs. Choose businesses you know well and would happily recommend on your own.

A good way to find partners is to ask: "Who does my customer call right before or right after they call me?"

## Examples that work

- **Home sales:** realtor, lender, home inspector, mover, cleaner
- **Home improvement:** contractor, interior designer, flooring, painter
- **Seasonal home care:** HVAC, pest control, pool service, landscaper
- **Small business startup:** accountant, attorney, IT consultant, marketer, insurance agent
- **Life events:** photographer, florist, event venue, caterer

## Set it up the right way

### Agree on the goal

Are you trying to reach new customers, reward existing ones or fill a slow season? A clear goal makes every other decision easier.

### Keep the offer simple

Customers should understand it in one sentence. "Book a fall HVAC tune-up and get a discount on pest control from our partner" is easy to understand. A complicated set of conditions is not.

### Define who does what

Decide up front who handles what: creating the flyer, posting on social media, tracking responses, answering customer questions and following up.

### Put it in writing

Even a short email summarizing the arrangement helps prevent misunderstandings. Include the offer, the start and end dates and each partner's responsibilities.

### Check the rules

Some professions have rules about referral fees, discounts and joint marketing. Real estate, insurance, financial, legal and health care professionals in particular should check what is allowed before launching anything.

## Promote it together

The real power of a joint offer is shared reach. Each partner should:

- Email the offer to their own customer list
- Share it on their social media and tag the partner
- Mention it in person to customers who fit
- Feature it on their website, if it makes sense

## Measure and follow up

When the offer ends, compare notes. How many customers responded? What worked and what did not? Was the partnership easy to run? A successful joint offer often becomes a regular seasonal tradition.

## The holiday season is a great time to start

With the holidays on the way, many customers are planning purchases, hosting family or getting their homes ready for guests. A well-timed joint offer from businesses they trust can stand out from the generic holiday noise.
