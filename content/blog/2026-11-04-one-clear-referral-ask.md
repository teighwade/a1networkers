Every week at BNI A1 Networkers, each member gets a short window to tell the room about their business. It is tempting to use that time to list everything you do. After all, more services should mean more chances for someone to think of you, right?

In practice, it usually works the other way. A long list blurs together, and by the time the next member stands up, nobody remembers any of it. The members who get the most referrals tend to ask for **one clear thing.**

## Why specific works better

When you say "I do insurance," everyone in the room nods and moves on. When you say "I am looking to meet families who just bought their first home in Peoria and have not reviewed their insurance since closing," something different happens. People start picturing a real person. Maybe it is a neighbor, a coworker or a client who just closed on a house.

Specific asks work because they:

- **Trigger a memory.** People think in stories and faces, not in categories.
- **Are easy to repeat.** Your fellow members can pass along one clear sentence.
- **Show expertise.** A focused ask tells people you know exactly who you help best.

## A simple formula for your weekly ask

Try building your ask from three parts:

1. **Who.** A specific type of person or business. "Homeowners in Sun City West," "new restaurant owners" or "real estate agents who list older homes."
2. **When.** The moment they need you. "Right after they move in," "before summer," "when they are hiring their first employee."
3. **How to bring it up.** A simple line your fellow members can use. "Ask them if they have had their AC checked this year."

Put together: *"This week I am looking for homeowners in Surprise whose AC is over 12 years old. If someone mentions a high electric bill, ask if they have had the system checked and connect them with me."*

## Rotate your focus

You do not have to choose one customer forever. Many members rotate their ask by season or by week. That keeps your presentations fresh, teaches the room about every part of your business over time and keeps people listening.

## Make it easy to say yes

Finish your ask with how a referral should reach you: a phone number, a text, or "just give them my card." The easier you make the handoff, the more likely it is to happen.

## Put it into practice

Before next Thursday, write down one specific customer you want to meet and one sentence a fellow member could use to bring you up. Then watch how the room responds.
