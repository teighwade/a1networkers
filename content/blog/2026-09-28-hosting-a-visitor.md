Walking into a BNI meeting for the first time can be intimidating. It is early, the room is full of people who already know each other and there is an agenda the visitor has never seen before. How members treat that guest in the first ten minutes often decides whether they ever come back.

The good news is that making someone feel welcome is not complicated. It just takes a little intention from everyone in the room, not only the visitor hosts.

## Before the meeting

Good hosting starts before Thursday morning.

If you invited a guest:

- **Send a reminder** the day before with the time, address and parking details
- **Tell them what to expect**, including that they will give a short introduction
- **Offer to meet them at the door** so they do not walk in alone
- **Let the visitor hosts know** they are coming, along with their name and profession

That last step matters. It allows the chapter to prepare a name badge and think about which members the guest should meet.

## The first five minutes

A visitor's first moments set the tone. When a guest walks in:

- Greet them by name if you know it, or introduce yourself if you do not
- Offer them coffee and show them where to sit
- Introduce them to two or three members who serve similar customers
- Explain briefly how the meeting will run

If you notice a visitor standing alone during open networking, walk over. Do not assume someone else will.

## Make introductions with purpose

The best introductions connect a visitor to someone with a natural reason to talk. If your guest is a mortgage lender, introduce them to the real estate agent and the insurance agent. If they run a landscaping company, introduce them to the pool service and the pest control member.

A helpful introduction sounds like:

> "Karen, this is Tom. He does residential painting, and a lot of his customers are the same homeowners you work with."

Now they have something to talk about right away.

## During the meeting

A visitor is watching how the chapter works. A few habits help them see it at its best:

- **Stay engaged.** Put your phone away and give your full attention to each presentation.
- **Keep your weekly presentation sharp.** Visitors notice when members are clear about what they do and who they want to meet.
- **Sit near the guest** if you invited them, so you can quietly explain what is happening.
- **Pass referrals with care.** This is where visitors see Givers Gain® in action.

## After the meeting

The meeting ending is not the end of the visit. Before your guest leaves:

- Ask what they thought and whether they have questions
- Make sure they connected with the visitor host
- Exchange cards and offer to set up a one-to-one

Then follow up within a day. A short message thanking them for coming and offering to answer questions goes a long way.

## Do not oversell

Welcoming someone is different from pressuring them. Let the meeting speak for itself. Answer questions honestly, including questions about the time commitment and what membership involves. The right members join because the fit is real, not because someone pushed.

## Why every member is a host

Visitor hosts play a key role, but the whole chapter shapes the experience. A visitor will remember the member who saved them a seat, the one who asked about their business and the one who sent a thoughtful follow-up text afterward.

Strong chapters grow because visitors feel that warmth. And every new member who joins brings a new set of connections, which means more referral opportunities for everyone.
