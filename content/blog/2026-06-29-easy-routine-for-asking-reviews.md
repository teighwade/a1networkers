Most local service businesses have plenty of happy customers. What many of them do not have is a steady stream of online reviews that shows it. The gap usually is not about quality. It is about asking.

People mean well. They tell you on the spot that you did a great job, and they mean it. Then they get in the car, the phone rings and the review never happens. The fix is not a big marketing campaign. It is a small, repeatable routine.

## Why reviews matter so much

When someone in Peoria, Glendale or Surprise searches for a plumber, a cleaner or an accountant, reviews are often the first thing they see. Reviews help in a few ways:

- **They build trust** with people who have never heard of you.
- **They help you show up** in local search results and on maps.
- **They tell your story** in your customers' words, which is more convincing than anything you can write about yourself.

Reviews also support your referrals. When a fellow BNI member recommends you, the person they referred will often look you up before calling. A healthy set of recent reviews backs up what your referral partner already said.

## Step 1: Pick the right moment

The best time to ask is when the customer is happiest, usually right after the job is done and they can see the result. For some businesses that is at the end of a service call. For others, it is when a project is delivered or a problem is solved.

Train yourself to notice the moment someone says "This looks great" or "Thank you so much." That is your cue.

## Step 2: Ask in person, briefly

Keep it simple and sincere. Something like:

> "I am really glad this worked out. Reviews make a big difference for a small business like mine. Would you be willing to leave a quick one on Google?"

Most people say yes. The ask itself is rarely the problem. The follow-through is.

## Step 3: Send the link the same day

Do not make people search for you. Have a direct review link ready to go and send it by text or email within a few hours, while the experience is fresh.

A short message works best:

- Thank them by name.
- Mention the specific job.
- Include the link.
- Keep it to two or three sentences.

## Step 4: Make it a habit, not a campaign

The businesses with the most reviews are usually not the ones that run a big push once a year. They are the ones that ask every customer, every time. A few ideas to make it stick:

- **Add it to your job checklist** so it happens with the final walkthrough or invoice.
- **Save your message as a template** on your phone.
- **Review your progress monthly.** If the number is not moving, look at where the routine is breaking down.

## Step 5: Respond to every review

Thank people who leave positive reviews, and respond calmly and professionally to anyone who is unhappy. Future customers read your replies too. A thoughtful response to a complaint can build as much trust as a five-star review.

## A few things to avoid

- **Do not offer payment or discounts** in exchange for reviews. Most review platforms prohibit it, and it undermines trust.
- **Do not ask only the customers you think will be glowing.** Asking everyone keeps your reviews honest and realistic.
- **Do not write reviews for yourself** or ask friends who never used your service.

## Give reviews, too

Here is a Givers Gain® angle that is easy to overlook. If you have used a fellow member's services and were happy, leave them a review. It takes two minutes and it is one of the most useful things you can do for another small business owner. When you start the week, ask yourself who you could review today.

## Start this week

Summer can be a slower stretch for some West Valley businesses, which makes it a good time to set up a routine before fall picks up. Create your review link, write your text template and ask your next three customers. That is all it takes to get the habit started.
