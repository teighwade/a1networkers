If you own a small business, you probably know the feeling. You start the day planning to send invoices, then a customer calls, then a supplier has a problem, then you remember you were supposed to post on social media. By 5:00 PM you have been busy all day and the invoices still are not sent.

Time blocking is a simple way to take back control. Instead of working from a to-do list and reacting to whatever comes up, you assign specific blocks of time to specific kinds of work.

## What time blocking is

Time blocking means scheduling your work on your calendar the same way you schedule meetings. A block might be "9:00 to 11:00, client projects" or "Friday 2:00 to 3:00, bookkeeping."

The goal is not to plan every minute. It is to protect time for the work that matters most, so it does not get crowded out by everything else.

## Why it works for owners

When you wear every hat, the urgent always beats the important. Time blocking helps because it:

- **Reduces switching.** Grouping similar tasks means less time lost jumping between them.
- **Protects growth work.** Sales, marketing and networking get dedicated time instead of leftovers.
- **Sets expectations.** Your team and your customers learn when you are available.
- **Shows you the truth.** Once you put your week on a calendar, you see exactly where the hours go.

## Start with your hats

Make a list of the roles you play every week. For most owners, it looks something like this:

- Doing the work (service, production, client projects)
- Sales and follow-up
- Marketing and networking
- Admin, billing and bookkeeping
- Planning and strategy
- Managing people

Next to each one, estimate how many hours per week it really needs.

## Build your ideal week

Now put those hours on a blank calendar. A few tips:

1. **Lock in fixed commitments first.** Your weekly networking meeting, standing client appointments and family time go on the calendar before anything else.
2. **Match tasks to your energy.** Put focused work when you are sharpest. For many people that is the morning.
3. **Batch the small stuff.** Return calls and emails in two or three set windows each day instead of constantly.
4. **Give admin a home.** One recurring block for invoicing and bookkeeping keeps it from piling up.
5. **Leave buffer time.** Something will always come up. An open block in the afternoon gives you room to absorb it.

## Example: a service business owner

Here is how one week might look for an owner who runs a home service company:

- **Monday morning:** Plan the week, review the schedule and check in with the team
- **Thursday 7:00 AM:** Weekly networking meeting
- **Weekday mornings:** Field work and customer appointments
- **Weekday afternoons, 3:00 to 4:00:** Return calls, send quotes, follow up on referrals
- **Tuesday afternoon:** One-to-one coffee with a referral partner
- **Friday afternoon:** Invoicing, bookkeeping and next week's plan

It does not have to be perfect. It just has to be better than reacting all day.

## Protect your networking time

Networking is often the first thing to slip when life gets busy, and it is usually the thing that keeps the pipeline full months from now. Treat your weekly meeting and one-to-ones like client appointments. Block them out, and do not move them unless you absolutely have to.

## Make it stick

Time blocking takes a few weeks to feel natural. To keep it going:

- Review your calendar every Friday and adjust the next week
- Turn off notifications during focus blocks
- Tell your team or family about your schedule
- Be forgiving when a day falls apart, and start fresh the next morning

## A good time to start

October is a great month to try this. The West Valley weather finally cools off, outdoor work gets easier and a lot of businesses get busier heading into the holidays. Setting up a better week now means you will head into November and December with a plan instead of a pile.
