Most small business owners know their customers well. Many know their suppliers and their competition. But ask a business owner when they last had a real conversation with their banker, accountant or insurance agent, and you may get a blank stare.

These three professionals can have a big effect on how your business runs, how well it is protected and how ready it is to grow. Building real relationships with them, before you need something urgently, is one of the smartest moves a local business owner can make.

## Your banker: more than a place to keep money

A lot of business owners only talk to their bank when something goes wrong or when they need a loan right away. By then, you are a stranger asking for a favor.

A banker who knows you and your business can:

- Explain options for lines of credit, equipment financing or business loans before you need them
- Help you set up accounts and services that fit how your business actually operates
- Give you a heads-up on tools that save time, like merchant services or online payments
- Speak up for you internally when a request needs a little extra explanation

**How to build the relationship:** Schedule a short meeting once or twice a year, even when you do not need anything. Share how the business is doing and what you are planning next.

## Your accountant: your best early warning system

Many business owners only see their accountant at tax time. That is a missed opportunity. A good accountant sees patterns in your numbers that you might not notice on your own.

An accountant who is involved throughout the year can help you:

- Plan for taxes ahead of time instead of being surprised in April
- Understand which parts of your business are actually profitable
- Make smarter decisions about hiring, equipment and pricing
- Keep clean records that make borrowing easier

Mid-September is a good moment for this conversation. There are still a few months left in the year, which means there is still time to act on any planning ideas before December.

**How to build the relationship:** Ask for a mid-year or fall check-in. Bring your questions and your plans for the coming year.

## Your insurance agent: protection that fits your business

Business insurance is easy to set up once and forget. But businesses change. You add employees, buy equipment, take on bigger jobs or start offering new services. If your coverage does not keep up, you could be underinsured without knowing it.

An insurance agent who knows your business can:

- Review whether your coverage still matches what you do
- Explain options like general liability, commercial auto, property and workers' compensation
- Help you understand what contracts and clients may require
- Walk you through what to do if you ever need to file a claim

**How to build the relationship:** Ask for a coverage review once a year, and call your agent whenever something big changes in your business.

## Why these relationships pay off

The common thread is timing. When you already have a relationship, you get better advice, faster answers and more context. When you call for the first time in a crisis, you are starting from zero.

These professionals also tend to be well connected. Bankers, accountants and insurance agents work with many local businesses, so they often know who else could help you. That makes them excellent referral partners.

## Let them know who you help, too

Relationships go both ways. Once your banker, accountant and insurance agent understand your business, they may be in a position to recommend you to their own clients. Tell them who your ideal customer is. You might be surprised how often they hear about someone who needs exactly what you offer.

## A simple plan for this fall

Before the year gets busier, try this:

1. Write down the name of your banker, accountant and insurance agent. If you do not have one of them, that is your first task.
2. Schedule a short check-in with each one before the end of the year.
3. Bring one question and one update about your business.

Small conversations now can prevent big problems later.
