A BNI chapter does not have a paid staff running things behind the scenes. The weekly meeting, the visitor welcome, the training moments and the follow-up all happen because members volunteer to take on a role. Those volunteers are the reason a chapter runs smoothly week after week.

If you have ever wondered who does what, here is an overview of common chapter roles and why members choose to serve.

## The leadership team

Most chapters are led by a small team of officers:

- **President.** Runs the weekly meeting, keeps the agenda on time and sets the tone for the chapter. The president also works with the rest of the team on the chapter's goals.
- **Vice President.** Often focuses on member accountability and chapter health, including attendance and how members are participating. The vice president also steps in when the president is away.
- **Secretary / Treasurer.** Handles the chapter's finances, such as meeting fees, and helps keep records in order.

## Support roles

Beyond the officers, chapters rely on several other volunteer positions. The exact list varies, but these are common:

- **Membership Committee.** Reviews applications, helps make sure new members are a good fit and keeps an eye on the overall health of the membership.
- **Visitor Hosts.** Greet guests, explain how the meeting works and answer questions afterward. For many visitors, the visitor host is their first impression of the chapter.
- **Education Coordinator.** Shares a short educational moment during the meeting, usually a practical tip on networking, referrals or BNI best practices.
- **Mentor Coordinator.** Pairs new members with experienced ones so they can get up to speed quickly.
- **Events Coordinator.** Organizes socials, special meetings and other gatherings that help members get to know each other outside the weekly agenda.
- **Social Media Coordinator.** Helps share the chapter's story online and highlight members.

## Why members volunteer

Taking on a role means extra time and effort. So why do members raise their hands? A few common reasons:

### It builds visibility

When you lead part of the meeting or greet every visitor, people get to know you. That familiarity often turns into trust, and trust turns into referrals.

### It builds skills

Running a meeting, coaching a new member or presenting an education moment are all chances to practice leadership and public speaking. Those skills carry right back into your own business.

### It deepens relationships

Leadership team members work closely together. Planning meetings and solving problems side by side creates stronger connections than a weekly handshake ever could.

### It is a way to give back

BNI runs on Givers Gain®. Serving in a role is one of the most direct ways to put that idea into practice. Members who have benefited from the chapter often want to make sure the next person has the same experience.

## Leadership terms keep things fresh

Leadership roles in BNI rotate on a regular schedule rather than being held by the same people forever. That gives more members a chance to serve, brings in new ideas and keeps any one person from burning out. It also means that, over time, many members get a turn helping lead the chapter.

## You do not have to be a born leader

Some roles are very public, like president. Others happen mostly behind the scenes. There is room for different personalities and schedules. New members often start with a smaller role, learn how the chapter works and grow from there.

## A chapter is only as strong as its volunteers

When you visit a well-run chapter, you can feel it: the meeting starts on time, visitors are welcomed warmly and members know what to expect. That is the work of volunteers who decided the chapter was worth their time.
