One of the most common worries for new BNI members goes something like this: "I want to help, but I do not have anyone who needs a plumber right now." You sit through the meeting, hear everyone's asks and feel like you have nothing to offer.

The truth is that a referral does not always have to be a customer with a checkbook. There are plenty of ways to help other members grow, even in a week when nobody you know is shopping for what they sell.

## Change how you think about a referral

A referral is any opportunity you pass along that helps someone's business. Sometimes that is a hot lead. But it can also be:

- An introduction to someone who serves the same customers
- A connection to a group, association or organization
- A recommendation to a vendor who could save them time or money
- A speaking or exposure opportunity
- Useful information about a project, opening or new development

In Givers Gain®, the spirit matters more than the size. Small, thoughtful referrals build the relationships that lead to big ones.

## Introduce members to referral partners

This may be the most valuable thing you can do. Instead of sending one customer, connect a member with someone who could send them customers again and again.

A few examples:

- Introduce a real estate agent to a property manager you know
- Connect an HVAC company with a home warranty rep or a remodeler
- Introduce a bookkeeper to a business coach or attorney
- Connect a photographer with an event planner or wedding venue

Each of these can lead to a steady stream of business long after your introduction.

## Use your own business as a referral

Sometimes you are the customer. If you need a service that a fellow member provides, hire them. Need new business cards, a tune-up on your AC before the summer is over, or a bookkeeper? Start with the room.

When you use a member's services, you also get firsthand experience. That makes your future referrals for them more confident and more specific.

## Open doors in your community

Think about the places you already spend time:

- Your HOA or neighborhood group
- Your church or community organizations
- Your kids' school and sports teams
- Industry associations and chambers of commerce

Many of these have needs that members can fill, such as a speaker for a meeting, a vendor for an event or a sponsor for a fundraiser. Late August is a good time to look, since school is back in session and a lot of fall events are being planned right now.

## Listen for trigger phrases

You often have referrals in your week without realizing it. Train yourself to notice when people say things like:

- "We just bought a house."
- "Our AC has been making a weird noise."
- "I am thinking about selling."
- "We are finally starting that remodel."
- "I need to get my business finances in order."

When you hear one, ask a follow-up question and offer to connect them with a member. That is a referral.

## Give testimonials and recommendations

If you have worked with a member and had a good experience, say so. Share it in the meeting, write an online review or post a recommendation on LinkedIn. Public praise from a real client carries a lot of weight and costs you nothing but a few minutes.

## Do one-to-ones with purpose

The better you understand a member's business, the more referrals you will spot. During a one-to-one, ask:

- Who is your ideal customer?
- What does a typical client say right before they call you?
- Who else do your clients usually hire around the same time?

Write the answers down. They become your list of things to listen for.

## A simple weekly goal

If you are not sure where to start, aim for one meaningful action each week:

1. Make one introduction between a member and a potential partner.
2. Hire or recommend one member's service.
3. Share one testimonial or review.

Over a few months, that adds up to dozens of chances to help.
