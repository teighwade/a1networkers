When you introduce your business, it is tempting to list everything you do. But ask anyone what they remember from a networking meeting, and it is rarely a list. It is a story: the homeowner whose roof was leaking into the nursery, the small business owner who almost lost all their files, the family who finally found the right coverage after a bad experience.

Stories stick. They help people picture who you help and remember you when they meet someone in the same situation. The best part is that a good customer story does not need much time. You can tell one in under a minute.

## Why stories work

Stories work because they:

- **Create a picture.** Listeners can see the customer, the problem and the result.
- **Show your value without bragging.** You are describing what happened, not claiming to be the best.
- **Help people refer you.** When a listener meets someone with the same problem, the story comes back to mind, and so do you.
- **Are easy to repeat.** A good story can be retold by someone else, which is exactly what you want from a referral partner.

## A simple structure

Most short success stories follow the same basic shape. Try this four-part formula:

1. **The customer.** Who were they? Keep it general and respect their privacy. "A retired couple in Sun City West" or "a new restaurant owner in Glendale."
2. **The problem.** What were they dealing with? What was at stake?
3. **What you did.** One or two sentences about how you helped. Skip the technical details.
4. **The result.** How did things turn out? How did the customer feel?

Then add one last line:

5. **The ask.** "If you know someone dealing with something similar, I would love to help them too."

That is it. Five pieces, under a minute.

## An example

Here is a sample story, using a made-up business for illustration:

*"Last month, a homeowner called me because her pool had turned green right before her daughter's birthday party. She had tried everything and was ready to cancel. We came out, figured out her pump timer had failed, got the water balanced and had it clear two days before the party. She sent me a picture of fifteen kids in the pool. If you know someone whose pool is giving them trouble, I would love to help them before their next big weekend."*

That takes about thirty seconds to say, and people will remember it.

## Tips for telling it well

- **Pick one story.** Do not try to squeeze in two. One clear story beats two rushed ones.
- **Be specific.** "Before a birthday party" is more memorable than "before an event."
- **Keep the jargon out.** Your listeners are not experts in your field.
- **Protect privacy.** Do not use real names unless you have permission. Check your industry rules if you work in a regulated field.
- **Practice out loud.** Time yourself. Most people talk longer than they think.
- **End with the ask.** A story without an ask is just entertainment.

## Build a story library

You probably have dozens of great customer stories. Start writing them down. Keep a simple note on your phone with a few lines for each one. Over time you will have a library you can draw from for networking meetings, social media posts, your website and sales conversations.

It helps to have stories for different situations:

- A story about a typical customer
- A story about an emergency or urgent fix
- A story about a long-term relationship
- A story about a referral that worked out well
