Thanksgiving is next Thursday, and once it passes, December tends to disappear. Holiday parties, family visits, year-end invoices and last-minute customer requests all compete for your time. Before you know it, January 1 shows up and you are starting the new year without a plan.

The good news is that a useful year-end review does not need a weekend retreat or a 40-page business plan. An hour or two with a notepad, your calendar and your sales records is enough. Doing it now, before the holiday rush peaks, means you can walk into 2027 knowing exactly what you want.

## Start with where your business came from

Pull a list of the jobs, clients or sales you closed this year and ask one question about each: how did this person find me?

You will probably see a few patterns. Maybe most of your best work came from referrals, a handful of repeat customers or one particular partner. Maybe your paid ads brought in a lot of calls but very few customers you would want again.

This is the single most valuable part of the review. When you know where your good business comes from, you know where to spend more time next year, and where to spend less.

## Look at your best customers, not just your biggest

Your biggest customers by dollars are not always your best ones. Look for the customers who paid on time, were easy to work with, appreciated your work and sent other people your way.

Write down what those customers have in common. Are they homeowners in a certain area? Small businesses of a certain size? People going through a certain life event, like buying a home or caring for an aging parent?

That description is gold. It becomes your referral ask for next year, because it tells your network exactly who to look for.

## Be honest about what did not work

Every business tries things that do not pay off. A marketing channel that ate money. A service you offered that nobody wanted. A type of job that always took twice as long as you quoted.

You do not need to beat yourself up about it. Just write it down and decide whether to fix it, change it or drop it. Clearing out what is not working frees up time and money for what is.

## Thank the people who helped

While you are looking back, make a short list of the people who sent you business or helped you out this year. Referral partners, loyal customers, a vendor who came through in a pinch.

The holidays are a natural time to reach out. A handwritten card or a quick phone call means more than most people expect, and it keeps those relationships strong going into the new year.

## Pick three goals for 2027

Resist the urge to write fifteen goals. Pick three that would make the biggest difference, and make them specific enough that you will know whether you hit them.

For example:

- **A revenue or job target**, broken down by month or quarter.
- **A relationship goal**, like holding two one-to-ones a month with referral partners.
- **A capacity goal**, like hiring help, raising prices or blocking off time for the work only you can do.

Then put the first step for each goal on your calendar for the first week of January. A goal with a date attached is much more likely to happen.

## Share your goals with your network

This is where being part of a referral group pays off. Your fellow members cannot help you reach goals they do not know about.

When the new year starts, tell your referral partners what you are aiming for and who your ideal customer is. If you want more commercial work, say so. If you want to grow in Surprise or Buckeye, say that too. Specific goals give people something to watch for.

It also works the other way. Ask your partners about their goals for 2027, and think about who you know that could help them get there. That is Givers Gain® in practice.

## Schedule your January one-to-ones now

January calendars fill up quickly too. Before December gets away from you, reach out to three or four people you want to know better and book a one-to-one for the first few weeks of the year. You will start 2027 with momentum instead of trying to build it from scratch.
