If you ask longtime BNI members where their referrals actually come from, many will point to the same thing: the one-to-one. It is a simple idea. Two members sit down for about 30 minutes, usually over coffee, and learn each other's businesses well enough to send real business back and forth.

The weekly meeting gets people familiar with you. The one-to-one is where they start to trust you. Here is how to make that half hour count.

## Pick the right person to start with

You can and should meet with everyone in your chapter eventually. But if you are just getting started, begin with people whose customers overlap with yours. A few natural pairings:

- A real estate agent and a lender, home inspector or title rep
- A plumber and a handyman, HVAC company or restoration company
- A health insurance agent and a payroll provider or accountant
- A carpet cleaner and a property manager or house cleaner

These are often called "power teams" or contact spheres. When you serve the same customer at different moments, referrals tend to flow in both directions.

## Prepare before you sit down

Thirty minutes goes fast. A little prep keeps it from turning into small talk.

1. **Look them up.** Visit their website and social pages. Know the basics before you arrive.
2. **Think about who you already know.** Is there a neighbor, client or friend who might need their service?
3. **Bring your own short list.** Have two or three specific types of customers in mind that you would like to meet.

It also helps to pick a spot that works for both of you. In the West Valley summer, that probably means somewhere indoors with good air conditioning and iced coffee, not a patio at noon.

## Use the time well

A simple structure keeps things balanced. Try splitting the time roughly in half and covering these topics for each person:

- **Their background.** How did they get into this line of work?
- **Their ideal customer.** Who is a great fit, and who is not?
- **Their trigger moments.** What does a customer say or do right before they need this service?
- **How to introduce them.** What is the one sentence you could say to a friend that would make the handoff easy?

Take notes. You will not remember the details a week later, and the details are what make a referral work.

## Ask the questions that lead to referrals

Some questions open doors better than others. A few that work well:

- "What is the best referral you have ever received? What made it great?"
- "What is a problem your customers have that they do not know you can solve?"
- "Who else do your customers usually hire before or after they hire you?"
- "If I hear someone say something specific, what should it be?"

That last one is gold. When a plumber tells you, "If someone mentions their water heater is making a popping sound, send them my way," you now have a concrete trigger you can listen for.

## End with a clear next step

Do not leave with a vague "let's keep in touch." Before you part ways, agree on something specific:

- An introduction you will make this week
- A second meeting to go deeper
- A shared customer type you will both watch for

Givers Gain® works best when someone takes the first step. Offering to make an introduction before you ask for one sets the tone for the whole relationship.

## Follow up within 24 hours

The follow-up is where most one-to-ones fall apart. Send a short thank-you message the same day. If you promised an introduction, make it right away while it is fresh. A quick group text or email works fine: "Jane, meet Bob. Bob is the plumber I mentioned. Bob, Jane just bought a house in Peoria and has a few questions."

Then add a reminder to check back in a month. Relationships grow through repeated contact, not one great conversation.

## Make it a habit

One one-to-one is nice. One a week is a system. Members who build a steady rhythm of these meetings tend to understand the whole room better and become the person everyone wants to refer.
