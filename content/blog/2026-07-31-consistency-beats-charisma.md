Picture the person you think of as a natural networker. They work the room, remember everyone's name and tell a great story. It is easy to assume that is what it takes to get referrals.

Here is the good news for the rest of us: charisma helps you make a first impression, but consistency is what earns trust. And trust is what makes someone hand you a referral.

## Referrals are about risk

When someone refers you, they are putting their own reputation on the line. If you do a great job, they look good. If you drop the ball, it reflects on them.

That is why people do not refer the most exciting person they met. They refer the person they are confident will show up, call back and do the work. Confidence like that is built over time, through dozens of small moments where you did what you said you would.

## What consistency looks like

Consistency is not glamorous. It looks like:

- **Showing up every week**, including the weeks when business is slow or you would rather sleep in
- **Arriving on time** and staying engaged for the whole meeting
- **Giving a clear, focused presentation** instead of winging it
- **Following up on referrals within a day**, every time
- **Doing one-to-ones regularly**, not just when you are new
- **Reporting back** to the person who referred you so they know how it went

None of these require a big personality. They just require a habit.

## The compounding effect

Think about how often you see someone in a weekly chapter. Over a year, that is around 50 meetings. Each one is a chance for the room to learn a little more about you and your business.

After a few months, members start to understand who you serve. After six months, they have seen you follow through. After a year, they know you well enough to recommend you without hesitation. The charismatic visitor who came once and never returned does not get that chance.

## Why charisma can backfire

Charisma without follow-through can actually hurt you. If someone makes a great first impression and then misses meetings, takes days to return calls or cannot explain what kind of referral they want, people notice the gap. The higher the first impression, the bigger the letdown.

Meanwhile, the quieter member who shows up every week, listens carefully and quietly delivers is building a reputation that lasts.

## Tips for introverts

If networking does not come naturally, consistency is your advantage. A few ideas:

- **Arrive a little early.** Smaller groups during open networking are easier than a full room.
- **Ask questions.** Most people enjoy talking about their business. You do not have to carry the conversation.
- **Prepare your presentation.** Write it down and practice. A clear message matters more than delivery.
- **Lean on one-to-ones.** A coffee conversation with one person is often more comfortable, and more valuable, than a crowded room.
- **Track your follow-ups.** A simple list keeps you reliable without relying on memory.

## Summer is the test

Late July in the West Valley is a real test of consistency. It is hot before the sun is fully up, families are traveling and plenty of people let their routines slip until fall. That is exactly why it matters to keep showing up.

The members who stay engaged through summer are the ones whose names come up when business picks back up in the fall. Their fellow members have not forgotten about them, because they never went anywhere.

## Build the habit

Pick two or three consistency habits and commit to them for the next 90 days:

1. Attend every meeting, or send a substitute when you cannot.
2. Return every referral call within 24 hours.
3. Book one one-to-one each week.

Track how it goes. Most people find that the referrals follow the habits, not the other way around.

## The bottom line

You do not need to light up a room. You need to be the person everyone knows they can count on. Show up, follow through and keep doing it, and your network will grow whether you are the life of the party or not.
