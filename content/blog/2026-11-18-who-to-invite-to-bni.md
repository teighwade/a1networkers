Every BNI chapter grows the same way: one visitor at a time. A stronger chapter means more professions in the room, more eyes looking out for your customers and more referrals moving around each week. That is why inviting guests is part of being a member, not an extra chore.

The hard part is usually not the invitation itself. It is knowing who to ask. Most of us scroll through our phones, see a hundred names and freeze. Here is a simpler way to think about it.

## Start with the people you already refer

Think about the last few times a customer asked you, "Do you know someone who does…?" Who did you send them to? Those business owners are already part of your referral network. You trust them, and your customers trust them because of you.

If their profession is not already represented in the chapter, they are a natural first invite. You are not asking them to join a club. You are offering them a room full of people who could send them the same kind of business you have been sending.

## Look at who shares your customers

Every business has neighbors. A roofer's customers also need gutters, solar, painters and insurance agents. A real estate agent's clients need movers, cleaners, inspectors and handymen. A bookkeeper's clients need payroll, banking and IT help.

Make a short list of the businesses that touch your customers right before or right after you do. Those are the people who make the strongest referral partners, and they are the ones who will get the most value from a visit.

## Notice the vendors you already pay

Your own vendors are an easy and often overlooked group. The person who prints your shirts, fixes your work truck, does your taxes or keeps your computers running already knows you as a customer. They see firsthand how you run your business.

Invite them with something like: "You have done great work for me, and I think the people in my networking group would want to know you." That is a compliment and an opportunity in the same sentence.

## Do not forget newer business owners

Someone who just opened their doors or went out on their own is often the most eager visitor in the room. They need relationships quickly, they are hungry to learn and they usually show up ready to work. Keep an ear open at church, at your kids' activities, on neighborhood Facebook groups and at local events for people who say they recently started something.

## Make the invitation easy to accept

A good invite is short, specific and personal. Compare these two:

- "You should come to my BNI group sometime."
- "We meet Thursday at 7:00 AM at Brothers Family Restaurant in Peoria. I would love to have you as my guest this week. Want me to save you a seat?"

The second one gives a day, a time, a place and a clear yes-or-no question. It is much harder to let that one drift.

A few other things that help:

- **Explain what to expect.** Let them know they will hear short member introductions, a featured presentation and get a chance to introduce themselves.
- **Tell them what to bring.** Business cards and a quick sentence about who their ideal customer is.
- **Offer to meet them at the door.** Walking into a room of strangers at 7 AM is easier with a familiar face waiting.

## Follow up both ways

After the meeting, check in with your guest. Ask what stood out and whether they met anyone they want to talk to again. Even if BNI is not a fit for them right now, the visit strengthened your relationship, and they now know a room full of people they can refer to.

Also let your fellow members know who you invited and why. A quick heads-up helps them connect with your guest during open networking and makes the visit feel welcoming instead of awkward.

## Inviting is a form of Givers Gain®

It is easy to think of inviting as something you do for the chapter. But every guest you bring is also a gift to the people around you. When you invite a great business owner, every member in the room gets a potential new referral partner. When the chapter grows, everyone's network grows with it.

So this week, pick two or three names from the groups above and send a personal invitation. You may be surprised how often the answer is yes.
