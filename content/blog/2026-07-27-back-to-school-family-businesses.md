In much of the country, back-to-school means September. In the West Valley, it means right now. Many Arizona districts start classes in late July or the first days of August, while it is still well over 100° outside. That early start reshapes the calendar for local families, and for the local businesses that serve them.

If you run a family business, or your customers are families, here is what this season tends to bring and how to make the most of it.

## What changes when school starts

**Schedules tighten overnight.** The loose summer routine disappears. Mornings are rushed, afternoons fill up with practices and activities, and parents have less flexibility to take calls or meet service appointments.

**Spending shifts.** Families are buying supplies, clothes, shoes, sports gear and electronics. Some budgets get tight for a few weeks, while other categories, like after-school care and tutoring, see a surge in demand.

**Homes get a reset.** A lot of parents use the first quiet weekday morning in months to tackle the projects they put off all summer: deep cleaning, repairs, organizing and the home service calls they kept meaning to make.

**Teen drivers hit the road.** For many families, a new school year means a new driver in the household, which brings questions about cars, maintenance and insurance.

## How family businesses can adapt

### Rethink your availability

If your customers are parents, the windows when they can talk to you change in August. Think about offering early morning, school-hours or evening appointment options. Let customers book online or by text so they do not have to call during a busy day.

### Plan around your own family

If you are a parent running a business, your own schedule changes too. Block out school drop-off and pick-up times now instead of trying to squeeze them in later. A realistic calendar is better than an ambitious one you cannot keep.

### Speak to the moment

Customers respond to messages that match what is happening in their lives. A short note that ties your service to back-to-school season tends to land better than a generic promotion. A few examples:

- A cleaning service offering a "kids are back in school" reset
- An auto shop reminding families to check tires and brakes before the school commute
- A financial or insurance professional encouraging a review when a teen starts driving
- A tutor, coach or child care provider reminding parents that spots fill up early

### Watch for the busy-season ripple

Some businesses get busier in August. Others get quieter for a few weeks while families adjust. If your business slows down, use the time to follow up with past customers, ask for reviews and plan your fall.

## The referral angle

Back-to-school season is full of conversations. Parents talk to each other at drop-off, on the sidelines of practices and in school pickup lines. Those conversations are full of needs:

- "We need someone to help with math this year."
- "Our car has been making a noise and I need it fixed before the commute starts."
- "I finally have time to deal with the house."

If you are part of a referral network, this is a great season to listen. When you know which trusted professionals handle each of those needs, you can turn a casual conversation into a genuinely helpful introduction. That is Givers Gain® in everyday life: you help the parent, you help a fellow business owner and you build goodwill that tends to come back around.

It also helps to tell your networking group exactly how back-to-school affects your business. If you want to meet parents of new teen drivers, or families who just moved into the area before the school year, say so. The more specific your ask, the easier it is for others to help.

## A quick August checklist

- Update your hours or booking options to fit school schedules
- Send one short, seasonal message to past customers
- Line up your own family logistics before the first day
- Tell your referral partners what kind of customer you want to meet this month
- Keep your eyes and ears open for needs you can refer out

## Make the most of the new season

The start of the school year is a fresh start for families, and it can be one for your business too.
