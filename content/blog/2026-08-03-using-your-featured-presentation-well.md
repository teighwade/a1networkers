Most weeks at a BNI meeting, members get a short window to talk about their business. It is enough for a quick reminder and a specific ask, but not much more. Then, every so often, it is your turn for the **featured presentation**, a longer slot (often around ten minutes) where the whole room focuses on you.

That time is valuable. It is your best chance to help a room full of referral partners truly understand what you do and who you help. Here is how to make the most of it.

## Remember who your audience is

Your featured presentation is not a sales pitch to the people in the room. Some of them may become customers, but that is not the main goal. The goal is to **train your referral partners**. You want every person to leave knowing:

- What you do, in plain language
- Who your ideal customer is
- What a good referral sounds like
- How to bring you up in a normal conversation

If people walk out entertained but unsure how to refer you, the presentation missed its mark.

## Pick one focus

Ten minutes goes faster than you think. Trying to cover every service you offer usually leads to a rushed talk that nobody remembers. Instead, choose one theme:

- One service you want more of
- One type of customer you want to meet
- One common problem you solve
- One misconception about your industry

You will get other featured presentations in the future. You do not have to fit everything into this one.

## A simple structure

Here is an outline that works for most businesses:

1. **Opening (1 minute).** Who you are, your business and a quick hook. A question, a surprising fact about your industry or a short story works well.
2. **The problem (2 minutes).** What situation are your customers in when they need you? Help the room picture it.
3. **How you help (3 minutes).** Explain what you do and what makes your approach different. Keep it concrete.
4. **A story (2 minutes).** Walk through a real example of a customer you helped. Keep personal details private, but make the situation clear.
5. **The ask (2 minutes).** Tell the room exactly who you want to meet and how to bring you up. End with your contact information.

## Tell stories, not specs

People remember stories much better than lists of features. Instead of "We offer full-service bookkeeping," try describing the business owner who came to you with a shoebox of receipts two weeks before a deadline, and what changed after you helped.

Stories also make it easier for members to recognize a referral. When they hear a friend describe the same situation, your story will come to mind.

## Show, do not just tell

If your business lends itself to it, bring something visual:

- Before and after photos
- A product sample people can hold
- A simple handout with your ideal referral on it
- A short demonstration

Keep slides minimal. A few clear images usually work better than slides packed with text.

## Make the ask impossible to miss

The most important part of your presentation is the end. Be specific. Name the type of person or business you want to meet, the moment they need you and the words a member could use to connect you.

If you have a "dream referral," a specific company or role you would love an introduction to, this is the time to say it. Someone in the room may know exactly who that is.

## Practice and time it

Rehearse out loud at least twice and time yourself. Most people run long the first time. Cut anything that does not help the room refer you. Finishing a minute early is far better than getting cut off before your ask.

## Follow up afterward

After your presentation, thank the room and follow up with anyone who asked a question or offered an idea. Many members find the week after a featured presentation is a great time to schedule one-to-ones, while your business is fresh in everyone's mind.
